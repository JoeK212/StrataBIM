v1.3.0 - 2026-10-20 - CU-BEMS replay: the dashboard plays back one week of measured office data with New York weather outdoors

STRATA now reads data/cubems_week.json (33 zones, 7 floors, every 10 minutes: HVAC, lighting, plug, temperature, humidity, light level) and data/nyc_weather.json (hourly Open-Meteo, Central Park) when served over http, and replays them Monday to Sunday. A Data source switch in the panel flips between CU-BEMS replay (the default when the files load) and the simulation; the choice is remembered. Opened straight from disk, or if the files are missing or malformed, it falls back to the simulation and says why. The one fetch in the page is limited to those two same-origin files.

The measured data has gaps, and none of them are presented as real readings. Found by analysing the actual week: temperature and humidity sensors sit on a flat forward-filled value until about Wednesday 3 PM, runs of 6 hours or more of an identical reading are treated as sensor holds, floor 1 and one zone per floor have no temperature sensor, and 9 zones lack an HVAC, lighting or plug meter. Temperature and humidity are filled from the zone's floor, then the whole building, then a fit to the building's own occupied and unoccupied readings, and are labelled estimated in the zone card and the Building note and never raise the Running warm alert. Unmetered loads show zero and are labelled not metered. People are inferred from lighting plus plug load (5th to 95th percentile), because the dataset has no occupancy counts. The reference office is now 4-4-5-5-5-5-5 zones per floor to match CU-BEMS, so each zone shows its namesake. Imported Revit zones borrow the CU-BEMS zone at the same position on the matching floor (floors wrap after seven) and scale it by floor area.

The weather is New York in October 2025 and the building is Bangkok in March 2019, so the outdoor layer is context only; the header, stats and Help say so. In replay mode the clock shows weekday and time with no calendar date, the temperature colour range is 18 to 30 C, and the Simulation section is split into Data source and Building. New test_data.py (32 checks over http) covers mapping, NaN and range safety, the 781 kW peak, estimate flags, source switching, an imported model, bad data files and file://.

v1.2.3 - 2026-10-05 - Fix stub-height blocks on stepped sites: a level now runs up to the next real storey, not the next nearby level

Found by loading the real Snowdon Towers export in Chrome. Its ground floor is split into blocks at -1.8, -1.05 and 0.0 m, and each level's height was taken from the gap to the next level in elevation order, so Block 35 and Block 37 got 0.75 m and 1.05 m gaps clamped up to 2.4 m. They extruded only 2.1 m tall and left a visible void under the L2 stack that looked like offset or floating blocks. A level's height is now the distance up to the next level at least 2 m higher, clamped to 2.4 to 6 m (4.27 m and 3.52 m for those two blocks). The viewer recomputes this from the level elevations on load, so existing strata_model.json files and the copy remembered in the browser render correctly without re-exporting; the Dynamo exporter uses the same rule so new exports match.

v1.2.2 - 2026-10-05 - Unconditioned spaces (roofs, shafts, stairs) draw no HVAC and never get faults; imported models open on the Density layer

A screenshot of the Snowdon Towers sample showed a green roof as one of the biggest power users and as the site of a planted cooling fault. Zones whose names match roof, shaft, stair, elevator, mechanical, chase, void, terrace, balcony, garage, parking, plenum, riser, exterior, outdoor, deck or canopy are now treated as unconditioned: no HVAC, about a tenth of the plug load, 30 percent lighting, almost no people, and an indoor temperature that follows the weather. An optional Room text parameter STRATA_Conditioned (Yes or No) overrides the name guess; the exporter carries it as an optional "conditioned" field, so existing strata-model-1 files still load. Planted faults are only placed in conditioned zones, the zone card says when a zone has no conditioning, and the Simulation section reports how many zones were treated that way. Loading a model now switches the colour layer from Power to Density if Power was selected, because raw kW paints the largest rooms amber regardless of how they are used. The reference building and generated buildings are unchanged.

v1.2.1 - 2026-10-05 - Add Density layer: power per unit of floor area, so large and small zones compare fairly

Raw kW colours the biggest rooms hottest regardless of how hard they work, which made imported Revit models with very different room sizes hard to read. Density divides each zone's power by its area (W/m² or W/ft² to match the unit toggle), has its own colour range sized to the building's week, shows beside the other readings in the zone card, and plots an area-weighted building figure in the trend chart. Power stays as it was.

v1.2.0 - 2026-10-05 - Load a Revit model: Dynamo Room exporter plus an in-browser importer that extrudes your rooms into coloured zones

tools/revit_export_rooms.py runs in a Dynamo CPython3 Python Script node (Revit Architecture has Rooms, not MEP Spaces). It reads every placed, enclosed Room, converts feet to metres, tessellates curved walls, keeps holes such as shafts, groups rooms into zones by an optional STRATA_Zone text parameter, then Department, then one zone per Room, and carries an optional STRATA_Sensor ID. It opens no transaction and changes nothing in the model. The output is a small strata-model-1 JSON file.

The viewer validates the file before using it (schema tag, level references, point counts, zone limit of 600, 25 MB limit), extrudes each room outline to its level height, merges a zone's rooms into one mesh, re-centres the model, rebuilds the floor strip with Revit level names, and reframes the camera. Readings on an imported model are the same simulated feed until real data is connected. Load with the button or by dropping the file on the model; the last model is remembered in the browser when small enough. Remove model returns to the reference office. data/sample_model.json is a synthetic four-level building with an L-shaped plan, a curved lobby and a shaft hole, for trying the loader without Revit.

The camera zoom limit, grid size and fog now scale with the model so large buildings are not clipped.

v1.0.0 - 2026-10-05 - Initial release: 3D 7-floor, 33-zone building viewer with simulated replay feed

First version of STRATA. Zones are boxes in a generated 56 m x 30 m plate, seven floors at 4 m pitch. Each zone is coloured by one of four layers: power, HVAC, temperature, occupancy. Data comes from a seeded generator (mulberry32) that is a pure function of (zone, minute), so scrubbing the timeline never depends on earlier playback state. The generator mimics the CU-BEMS channels (AC, lighting, plug kW, temperature, humidity, lux) so the real dataset can replace it without UI changes.

Includes: play/pause, four replay speeds, one-week scrubber, floor isolation strip, click-to-inspect with 24 h trend chart, planted HVAC faults (Wednesday night stuck cooling, Thursday afternoon cooling failure) feeding an alerts list, imperial and metric display with the other unit shown beside temperature, area and light level, Axis BIM Dark default with a light toggle, iOS safe-area and overscroll handling, seed persistence in localStorage.

Outdoor temperature is a placeholder sine curve, not real NYC weather. Zone counts per floor (5,5,5,5,5,4,4) are a placeholder for the real CU-BEMS zone map.

Found and fixed before release: the alerts list was rebuilt with innerHTML on every 4 Hz refresh, which detached the button between pointerdown and pointerup and could drop a tap. The list now rebuilds only when the set of alerting zones changes and updates the readout text in place.
